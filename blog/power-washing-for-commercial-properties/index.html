Power Washing for Commercial Properties: Where It Makes the Biggest Difference

This guide is written for Bay Area office managers, property managers, landlords, investors, and preservation/vendor contacts who need practical cleaning decisions rather than generic advice.

Identify the surface and the problem

Concrete, coated surfaces, masonry, and wood do not respond to the same cleaning method. Send photos of the surface, damage, and stains. Explain whether the aim is removing ordinary buildup, addressing a particular stain, or preparing an entrance for an event.

Plan pedestrian access and water handling

Identify water access, drains, loading areas, landscaping, and entrances that must stay usable. Agree on the work window and how people will be kept clear of wet work areas. The contractor should confirm an appropriate runoff plan for the site.

Set realistic expectations for stains

A photograph cannot guarantee a stain will come out. Ask whether a small test area is appropriate, what results are reasonable, and whether damaged coatings need a different approach. A written scope should name the areas being cleaned and any exclusions.
